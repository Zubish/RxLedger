1. DEFINE AND ACCEPT THE PRODUCT BASELINE

Document the critical pharmacy workflows: workspace setup, staff access, stock
receipt, batch handling, sale, return/adjustment, reporting and patient follow-up.
For each, identify the record owner, branch scope, permitted actors and outcome.
Record plan-dependent behaviour separately from basic correctness requirements.

A visual baseline should state that the original purple gradient lightning mark
is the RxLedger product logo. Keep pharmacy-uploaded branding as a separate concern.
List its placements: landing header/footer, onboarding, public authentication,
initial app preparation, workspace loading and favicon.

2. PRIORITY REQUIREMENTS TO ADAPT AND ACCEPT

RX-01 Workspace and branch isolation
Expected outcome: staff can access and act only within their permitted scope.
Acceptance: test branch switching, refresh, expired sessions and direct API requests
for a different branch/workspace. Reports, alerts and histories follow the same
accepted scope rules. Administrator exceptions must be explicit.

RX-02 Stock and batch integrity
Expected outcome: receipt, sale, issue, return and adjustment update stock and
movement history consistently under the accepted unit-conversion rules.
Acceptance: test repeated submissions, concurrent activity, interrupted writes and
mixed pack/sellable units. Define whether and when negative stock is permitted.
An operation must not leave its stock effect and recorded movement disagreeing.

RX-03 Expiry and dispensing rules
Expected outcome: the app distinguishes expired, near-expiry and sellable stock
using explicit date rules. FEFO recommendations reflect eligible available batches.
Acceptance: test boundary dates, zero quantities, branch scope and overrides.
Confirm the actual policy for expired stock; do not treat a sorting hint as a
substitute for that policy. Define relevant time zone and date interpretation.

RX-04 Sales and financial traceability
Expected outcome: receipts, sales history, stock effects and reports agree.
Acceptance: verify totals, discounts/taxes where supported, payment states,
returns/voids and retry handling against accepted accounting rules. Corrections
must remain explainable rather than silently rewriting operational history.

RX-05 Patient continuity
Expected outcome: pending medicines, follow-up and fulfilment states represent
real work and remain available only to permitted users.
Acceptance: transitions, repeat actions and branch transfers preserve ownership
and appropriate history. Define how completion is confirmed and who may confirm it.

RX-06 Alert preferences and scope
Expected outcome: clearing or muting an alert has the intended effect for the
intended user/branch and does not alter unrelated preferences.
Acceptance: switch branches, refresh and sign back in; confirm the accepted
preference scope and distinction between acknowledging an alert and fixing its cause.

RX-07 Consistent product identity
Expected outcome: all RxLedger logo placements use the accepted original artwork.
Acceptance: inspect landing, entry, sign-in/sign-up, loading/refresh and favicon on
mobile and desktop; pharmacy-specific branding continues to use its own asset.
Record the asset source and reusable component so later redesigns preserve it.

RX-08 External prescription exchange, if enabled
Expected outcome: incoming information is distinguishable from pharmacist review
and actual supply. Retry does not create duplicate business operations.
Acceptance: test source identity, mapping, status ownership, outage and duplicate
requests. Confirm the current HealthPass contract before changing it.

3. RXLEDGER-SPECIFIC IMPACT ANALYSIS

Trace stock-related changes across:
  receiving -> batch balance -> POS/issue -> movement ledger -> reports -> alerts.

Trace access changes across:
  account/workspace -> branch selection -> server authorisation -> queries -> UI.

Avoid fixing a visible number in isolation. A reporting mismatch may originate in
unit conversion, reversal logic, scope or historical records. Investigate the source
before changing a formula or hiding a discrepancy.

4. VERIFICATION AND RELEASE EVIDENCE

Maintain a compact set of synthetic pharmacy scenarios:
- Receive a batch, sell some units and reconcile remaining stock with its history.
- Complete an accepted return/adjustment and reconcile the result.
- Attempt an unauthorised branch/workspace request and confirm rejection.
- Exercise expiry boundaries and batch eligibility using controlled dates.
- Retry an interrupted transaction and confirm no duplicate stock or sales effect.
- Refresh a protected route and confirm session/navigation behaviour.

For visual changes, inspect only the relevant placements and screen sizes. For
transactional changes, verify records and reports as well as the interface.
Release notes should identify changed rules and any action pharmacy staff must take.

5. FIRST THREE IMPLEMENTATION CYCLES

Cycle 1 — Baseline the pharmacy rules
Confirm unit, expiry, reversal, scope and permission rules. Link them to code and
current checks. Record which critical paths lack evidence.
Completion evidence: accepted rule list and workflow/check matrix.

Cycle 2 — Protect operational integrity
Prioritise scope boundaries and stock/sales reconciliation. Fix recurring defects
at their source and add meaningful checks for the failure modes discovered.
Completion evidence: reconciliation scenarios and resolved high-impact gaps.

Cycle 3 — Improve rollout and support
Rehearse recovery, verify external contracts and refine staff-facing error guidance.
Pilot significant changes with a limited workspace where practical.
Completion evidence: recovery results, release record and updated support notes.

6. MAINTENANCE PRIORITY AND PROGRESS

Prioritise unauthorised access, incorrect stock or transaction effects and lost data
before cosmetic work. Next address blocked pharmacy workflows and recurring
support problems. Use preventive work to simplify repeatedly fragile shared rules.

Suggested measures: transaction success, stock/ledger discrepancies, duplicate
operations, branch-scope defects, repeated alert issues and recovery time. Confirm
how each measure is calculated before relying on it for decisions.

WORKING RULES FOR DEVELOPMENT

Use this guide as a living working document. Proposed requirements become accepted
baselines only after the product owner confirms them. Record disagreements and
exceptions; do not silently reinterpret a requirement to fit existing code.

For meaningful changes, use this brief:
  Change ID / date / owner:
  Problem and evidence:
  User outcome and acceptance criteria:
  Affected screens, services, records and external systems:
  Existing behaviour to preserve:
  Risk and dependencies:
  Implementation and verification approach:
  Deployment target and recovery approach:
  Result, remaining limits and links to evidence:

Scale the process to the change. A wording or isolated visual edit needs focused
review. A permission, data, transaction or integration change needs impact analysis
and tests of the affected boundaries. Destructive changes need validated recovery
arrangements. Avoid adding approval meetings for routine reversible work.

For AI-assisted work:
1. Read repository guidance, current requirements and the relevant implementation.
2. Trace the existing workflow and dependencies before editing it.
3. Separate verified facts, hypotheses and proposed requirements.
4. Implement the smallest suitable change; preserve unrelated working behaviour.
5. Verify the actual outcome, including affected failure paths.
6. Report what changed, what was tested, what remains uncertain and what is live.
7. Update durable project records when a material decision changes.

Keep authoritative knowledge in the repository rather than only in chat history.
Never put secrets, production records or credentials into prompts, examples,
release notes or committed environment files. Record configuration names and
purposes separately from their secret values.

MINIMUM PROJECT RECORDS

Start with a few concise files, combining sections where useful:
- Product and requirements: users, scope, critical workflows and acceptance criteria.
- Architecture and configuration: modules, data ownership, dependencies, environment
  purposes, API contracts and controlled items.
- Decisions: date, decision, reason, alternatives and consequences.
- Operations and releases: setup, verification, deployment, recovery and support.
- Backlog: defects, feature requests, maintenance risks and known limitations.

A release record should identify the exact commit, deployment, environment, schema
or migration state, relevant configuration changes, verification results, date and
person responsible. A code version alone does not describe the complete system.

REPEATABLE DELIVERY PROCEDURE

Request -> understand -> assess impact -> implement -> verify -> release -> observe.

Before release:
- Compare the change with its acceptance criteria and behaviours to preserve.
- Run checks suited to the risk: build/static checks, focused functional checks,
  integration tests and regression checks as needed.
- Keep test fixtures synthetic or appropriately de-identified and access-controlled.
- Check environment compatibility and data-change implications.
- Identify recovery actions; redeploying old code does not undo changed data.

After release:
- Confirm that the intended version is actually serving the intended audience.
- Exercise the affected user workflow and inspect relevant operational signals.
- Record defects and limitations; do not describe an unverified result as verified.
- Close the change only after its outcome and release state are recorded.

For a formal exception, record the reason, affected requirement, risk, owner,
compensating measure and review/expiry date. The course distinguishes a deviation
(authorised departure generally before the activity) from a waiver (acceptance of
an existing nonconformance). Neither should become a permanent invisible shortcut.

MAINTENANCE AND PROGRESS REVIEW

Classify work as corrective (repair a fault), adaptive (respond to an external
change), perfective (improve value or usability), or preventive (reduce future
failure or maintenance cost). Review all four categories when planning work.

At each working review, ask:
- What user outcome improved, and what evidence supports that?
- What is live, pending, blocked or deliberately deferred?
- Which defect or support issue keeps recurring?
- Which dependency or integration change needs preparation?
- What is the smallest preventive action that will reduce repeated effort?

Track a small number of useful measures: successful critical workflows, escaped
regressions, failed releases, recovery time, repeated support problems and time
from accepted request to verified delivery. Establish an initial measurement
period before choosing targets. Do not treat commit counts as product progress.

REFACTORING, MIGRATION AND RETIREMENT

Understand behaviour before restructuring it. Capture important behaviour with
appropriate checks, then improve one boundary at a time. Share code across apps
only when its purpose and contract are stable; similar screens do not necessarily
have identical business rules.

For migration: map old and new data meanings, validate relationships and ownership,
rehearse on an isolated environment, plan concurrent writes, compare important
counts/totals and use pilot or phased rollout where appropriate. Define cutover,
reconciliation, backup restoration and recovery limits before moving real users.

For retirement: identify users and dependencies, preserve required records, arrange
replacement/export access, communicate the transition, withdraw integrations and
unused access, and verify continuity. Retention and privacy requirements must be
established for the actual data and jurisdictions; this course summary does not
establish legal compliance.
